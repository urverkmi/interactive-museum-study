# Participant information: Reading about artworks online

**Who we are.** This study is run by Michelle Chang and Yuxin Zhang at KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Sweden, as a project for the course II2202 Research Methodology and Scientific Writing. Contact: michang@kth.se (Michelle Chang) or yuxz@kth.se (Yuxin Zhang). The data controller is KTH Royal Institute of Technology, data protection officer: dataskyddsombud@kth.se.

**What the study is about.** We are studying how the way a museum text is written affects what people remember from it and how they experience reading it. The results will help museums write texts that work for more visitors.

**What you will do.** You will see three paintings from museum collections, one after another, each with a short text. After each one you will answer a few questions about what you remember and how easy the text was. At the end there are four short questions. It takes about 12–15 minutes on a laptop or desktop computer. Please do not take notes, take screenshots or look anything up.

**Content note.** One painting, from 1563, shows a tavern and brothel scene with drinking and sexual behaviour. Nothing in it is explicit.

**Who can take part.** Anyone aged 18 or over who can read English. You do not need to know anything about art.

**Taking part is voluntary.** You can stop at any time by closing the window, without giving a reason. Answers given up to that point are kept as an incomplete session and are only counted as a dropout. If you finish and later want your answers removed, email us the participant code shown on the last page. Once the course project is complete and submitted, removal may no longer be possible.

**What we record**

| What | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Whether English is your first language and, if not, how well you read it | To describe who took part |
| Your answers to the memory questions, ratings and short written comments | To compare the texts |
| How long each page was open, and when the page was hidden or in the background | To measure reading time and spot interruptions |
| What you clicked: *Learn more*, and the parts of a painting you selected | To see how people use the texts |
| Device type, window size and browser | To check that the study worked on your screen |
| The date and time of each step | To link the steps above in order |

We do not ask for your name. Your answers are stored under a random code. We do not store your IP address. Please do not write your name or other personal details in the comment boxes.

**Optional interview.** At the end you can leave an email address if you would like to take part in a 20-minute online interview. Your email is stored separately and cannot be linked to your answers. We delete it once the course project is graded and the interviews, if any take place, are finished. If you are interviewed, you will be asked for consent again.

**How we use and keep the data.** Only the research team can see the raw data. It is stored in a Google Sheet, accessible only to the research team. This is a student project for course II2202 and is not intended for publication; results are reported only as group summaries in the course assignment. Short comments may be quoted, without anything that could identify you. The data is not shared outside the course, and it is deleted once the course project is graded.

**Legal basis and your rights.** We process your data as research in the public interest (GDPR Article 6(1)(e)). You have the right to ask what data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY, imy.se). This study is a course project for II2202 and is not subject to review under the Swedish Ethical Review Act, because it does not process sensitive personal data or involve physical intervention, and is not intended for publication.

**The artworks and texts.** The paintings belong to the KMSKA in Antwerp, the Carmen Thyssen Collection in Madrid and the City of Prague. One text for each painting is taken word for word from that museum's website. The other texts are our own simplified versions, written from the museum texts; the museums did not write or approve them. The paintings show their own times, including a colonial-era view of Martinique and the end of serfdom in Russia; we show them for research, not to endorse those views. Full credits are on the [Sources page](../sources/).

**Use of AI.** The simplified texts were first drafted with the help of an AI system (Claude, made by Anthropic) and then checked and edited by the research team against the museum texts.
